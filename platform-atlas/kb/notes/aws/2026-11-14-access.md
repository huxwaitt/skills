# Amazon Web Services: access, identity, roles, governance and commercial model (as of 2026-11-14)

## 1. Getting access
- **AWS Organizations is the tenancy hierarchy.** An organization has one administrative root, one management account, zero or more member accounts and nested organizational units (OUs); an SCP attached to the root applies to all OUs and member accounts but not to the management account, while a declarative policy on the root also applies to the management account. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/organizations/latest/userguide/orgs_getting-started_concepts.html
- **The AWS account is the unit of quota, billing and Region-scoped resources.** AgentCore and Bedrock quotas are per account per Region unless noted, so a client engagement normally gets one or more dedicated workload accounts inside the client's organization. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/bedrock-agentcore-limits.html
- **Bedrock model access is on by default in commercial Regions, with no access-request step.** Most models activate on first invocation if the caller has `aws-marketplace:Subscribe`, `aws-marketplace:Unsubscribe` and `aws-marketplace:ViewSubscriptions`, plus a valid payment method; the "Model access" page now applies only to AWS GovCloud (US). Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-access.html
- **Anthropic models older than Claude Opus 4.7 need a one-time First Time Use (FTU) form.** You submit it in the console or with the `PutUseCaseForModelAccess` API, once per account or once at the organization's management account, and member accounts inherit it; the form isn't required for Anthropic models called through the `bedrock-mantle` endpoint. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-access.html
- **The first model invocation sets up the Marketplace subscription in the background.** Calls may succeed for up to 15 minutes and then fail with `AccessDeniedException` if a prerequisite is missing; after the fix, allow up to 2 minutes. Check status with `GetFoundationModelAvailability` (`agreementAvailability`). Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-access.html
- **Some models need additional account-level access that activation doesn't grant.** Access is decided per account, so accounts in the same organization can differ, and AWS Sales must be contacted if `AccessDeniedException` persists. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-access.html
- **Organizations that must review a EULA before use should block models first.** AWS recommends blocking model access with SCPs or IAM, reviewing the third-party EULA, then allowing access; invoking a third-party model counts as accepting its EULA. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-access.html
- **Programmatic activation uses CLI or SDK calls.** The sequence is `aws bedrock list-foundation-model-agreement-offers --model-id <id>`, then `aws bedrock create-foundation-model-agreement --model-id <id> --offer-token <token>`; `offerType` `ALL` includes private offers. Source: https://repost.aws/knowledge-center/bedrock-resolve-marketplace-permission ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-access.html
- **Third-party Bedrock model private offers should be accepted from the management account or a delegated administrator.** Otherwise the offer can't be distributed through AWS Marketplace, and member accounts are billed public rates until AWS License Manager grants are created. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/managed-entitlements-subscribing.html
- **Day-one asks for an engineer joining a client.** Ask for:
  - an IAM Identity Center user and permission set in the target workload account(s);
  - the target Region(s) and any Region-deny SCP;
  - confirmation that Bedrock model prerequisites are done (Marketplace permissions, Anthropic FTU, payment method);
  - quota levels for the chosen models;
  - VPC, subnet and security group IDs if VPC mode is required;
  - the corporate IdP discovery URL and client IDs for AgentCore JWT inbound auth.

  (secondary: synthesis of the sources in this note) Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-access.html ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/singlesignon/latest/userguide/howtosessionduration.html
- **Amazon Quick Suite was renamed twice and is now AWS Quick.** It launched as Amazon Quick Suite (GA, What's New October 2025), and the rename from Amazon Quick to AWS Quick was posted 2026-11-09. Quick Sight is unchanged, and customers don't need to take any action. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/amazon-quick-aws-quick/ ; https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2025/10/amazon-quick-suite-agentic-ai-powered-workspace/
- **AWS Quick has a free trial.** The 30-day trial covers up to 25 users per account. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/build-ai-powered-employee-onboarding-agents-with-amazon-quick/

## 2. Identity and authentication
- **IAM Identity Center is the human SSO layer.** It federates an external IdP over SAML and provisions users and groups over SCIM 2.0 (Okta supports create, update, deactivate, group push and import). Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/singlesignon/latest/userguide/gs-okta.html
- **Microsoft Entra ID SCIM provisioning to IAM Identity Center doesn't unpack nested groups.** Only direct members of assigned groups are provisioned (dynamic groups do work), and attribute removals in Entra ID don't sync. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/singlesignon/latest/userguide/idp-microsoft-entra.html
- **IAM Identity Center permission-set session duration defaults to 1 hour and can be set up to 12 hours.** Identity Center creates an IAM role per permission set in each assigned account, with a 12-hour maximum session. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/singlesignon/latest/userguide/howtosessionduration.html
- **The AWS access portal session is a separate setting.** It is a global IAM Identity Center setting from 15 minutes to 90 days. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/define-a-custom-session-duration-and-terminate-active-sessions-in-iam-identity-center/
- **IAM Identity Center trusted identity propagation carries the end-user identity to AWS services.** An external OIDC IdP is registered as a trusted token issuer, and a customer managed application authorizes the token exchange for a specific `aud`; services such as Quick and Amazon Q Business then authorize by user or group and may log the user in CloudTrail. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/singlesignon/latest/userguide/trustedidentitypropagation-overview.html ; https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/configuring-amazon-q-business-with-aws-iam-identity-center-trusted-identity-propagation/
- **STS role sessions run from 900 seconds to the role's maximum.** The role maximum is configurable from 1 to 12 hours, and `DurationSeconds` defaults to 3600. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/cli/latest/reference/sts/assume-role-with-saml.html
- **STS role chaining caps the session at 1 hour.** The limit can't be raised; `AssumeRole` fails if `DurationSeconds` is over 3600 when chaining, which matters for long-running agent jobs. Source: https://repost.aws/knowledge-center/iam-role-chaining-limit
- **STS session policies are limited in size and scope.** You can pass one inline policy and up to 10 managed policy ARNs, with plaintext of at most 2,048 characters, and the result is the intersection with the role's policies. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/cli/latest/reference/sts/assume-role-with-saml.html
- **GitHub Actions keyless federation uses an IAM OIDC provider.** Create the provider for `https://token.actions.githubusercontent.com` with client ID `sts.amazonaws.com`, and use a role trust policy with `sts:AssumeRoleWithWebIdentity` and conditions on `token.actions.githubusercontent.com:aud` and `token.actions.githubusercontent.com:sub`. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/securityagent/latest/userguide/sample-cicd-github.html
- **IAM rejects GitHub OIDC trust policies that don't restrict `sub`.** A policy without a `token.actions.githubusercontent.com:sub` (or `job_workflow_ref`) condition, or one whose value is only wildcards, fails on create or update. When `sub` names a GitHub environment, also restrict `ref`. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/id_roles_create_for-idp_oidc.html ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/terraform-aws-provider-best-practices/security.html
- **Google Cloud workloads can assume AWS roles without creating an IAM OIDC provider.** Use `"Principal": {"Federated": "accounts.google.com"}` with `StringEquals` on `accounts.google.com:aud` (maps to the token's `azp`), `accounts.google.com:oaud` (maps to `aud`) and `accounts.google.com:sub`. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/access-aws-using-a-google-cloud-platform-native-workload-identity/
- **The IAM OIDC provider and the role must be in the same account.** A wrong provider ARN in `AssumeRoleWithWebIdentity` returns `InvalidIdentityToken ... No OpenIDConnect provider found in your account`. Source: https://repost.aws/knowledge-center/iam-oidc-idp-federation
- **IAM Roles Anywhere gives non-AWS workloads temporary credentials for X.509 certificates.** It uses a trust anchor (your CA or AWS Private CA), roles that trust the Roles Anywhere service, and profiles that scope down permissions. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/help-panel/rolesanywhere/latest/console/hp-roles-anywhere.html
- **IAM outbound identity federation lets AWS workloads authenticate to other clouds and SaaS without stored secrets (announced 2025-11-19).** A principal with `sts:GetWebIdentityToken` gets an AWS-signed JWT that the external service verifies against a JWKS endpoint. Token lifetime is 60 to 3600 seconds (default 300). The feature is enabled per account under IAM Account settings and has no extra cost. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/simplify-access-to-external-services-using-aws-iam-outbound-identity-federation/
- **IAM outbound federation can be restricted and has a usage limit.** The condition keys are `sts:IdentityTokenAudience`, `sts:DurationSeconds` and `sts:SigningAlgorithm`. The tokens can't be used for OIDC federation back into AWS through `AssumeRoleWithWebIdentity`. The enable call is the `EnableOutboundWebIdentityFederation` API (PowerShell `Enable-IAMOutboundWebIdentityFederation`). Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/simplify-cross-partition-access-with-aws-iam-outbound-identity-federation/ ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/id_roles_providers_outbound_getting_started.html ; http://docs.aws.amazon.com/powershell/v4/reference/index.html?page=Enable-IAMOutboundWebIdentityFederation.html&tocid=Enable-IAMOutboundWebIdentityFederation
- **Amazon Bedrock API keys exist for tools that only accept a bearer token.** Short-term keys inherit the generating principal's IAM permissions and last up to 12 hours or the session length, whichever is shorter. Long-term keys create an IAM user with `AmazonBedrockLimitedAccess` and an expiry from 1 day to never. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/accelerate-ai-development-with-amazon-bedrock-api-keys/
- **AWS recommends STS credentials over Bedrock API keys.** Use short-term keys only when SigV4 isn't possible, and use SCPs to deny creating or using keys if they aren't needed; `aws-bedrock-token-generator` (Python) and `@aws/bedrock-token-generator` (JS) mint short-term keys from existing credentials. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/securing-amazon-bedrock-api-keys-best-practices-for-implementation-and-management/ ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/api-keys-generate.html
- **AgentCore Identity is AWS's agent identity offering.** Amazon Bedrock AgentCore Identity is built for AI agents and automated workloads; each agent identity is a workload identity with agent-specific attributes, kept in a workload identity directory (`workload-identity-directory/default`). Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/identity.html
- **AgentCore Runtime creates a workload identity for each deployed agent automatically.** Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/propagate-user-authorization-context-in-ai-agents-with-amazon-bedrock-agentcore/
- **The AgentCore token vault stores OAuth tokens and API keys for agents.** It stores agent OAuth client credentials, users' third-party OAuth tokens (after consent) and API keys, refreshes expired tokens, and re-prompts the user when needed. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/agentcore/faqs/
- **Agent code calls AgentCore Identity for workload access tokens.** It calls `GetWorkloadAccessTokenForJWT` (production: AgentCore validates the end user's IdP JWT signature and expiry and keys stored credentials to `iss` and `sub`), `GetWorkloadAccessTokenForUserId` (a caller-supplied user ID string with no IdP verification) or `GetWorkloadAccessToken`. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/get-workload-access-token.html
- **AgentCore outbound OAuth supports two flows.** They are `M2M` (client credentials) and `USER_FEDERATION` (three-legged, authorization code). Each OAuth2 credential provider gets a unique callback URL (`https://bedrock-agentcore.<region>.amazonaws.com/identities/oauth2/callback/<uuid>`), returned by `CreateOauth2CredentialProvider`, which you register with the IdP afterwards. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/secure-ai-agents-with-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-identity-on-amazon-ecs/ ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/identity-idp-notion.html
- **AgentCore Identity features added in 2026.**
  - On-Behalf-Of (OBO) token exchange (April 2026 release notes).
  - Private Key JWT client authentication, with the key held in AWS KMS and RS256, PS256 or ES256 (July 2026).
  - Referencing existing AWS Secrets Manager secrets (July 2026).
  - A Consent Portal (September 2026).
  - VPC egress to private IdPs (April 2026).

  Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/release-notes.html
- **An AgentCore Runtime or Gateway uses either SigV4 (IAM) or OAuth (JWT) inbound auth, not both.** The choice is made at creation. JWT invocation is a plain HTTPS request with `Authorization: Bearer`; AWS SDKs don't manage it. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/resource-based-policies.html ; https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/industries/automate-customer-complaint-classification-with-ai-agents-on-aws/
- **The AgentCore Runtime JWT authorizer checks four configurable fields.** `discoveryUrl` must match `iss`, `allowedClients` must match `client_id`, `allowedAudience` must match `aud`, and the optional `allowedWorkloadConfiguration` restricts callers to your gateway; `customClaims` can enforce claim values (for example `CONTAINS_ANY`). Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/runtime-oauth.html ; https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/propagate-user-authorization-context-in-ai-agents-with-amazon-bedrock-agentcore/
- **IAM-authenticated AgentCore Runtime calls pass the user identity in a header.** Calls signed with IAM SigV4 propagate the user through `X-Amzn-Bedrock-AgentCore-Runtime-User-Id`. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/industries/automate-customer-complaint-classification-with-ai-agents-on-aws/
- **AgentCore Gateway inbound auth has four types.** They are `CUSTOM_JWT` (OIDC or RFC 8414 discovery URL plus allowed clients), IAM (caller needs `bedrock-agentcore:InvokeGateway` scoped to the gateway ARN), `AUTHENTICATE_ONLY` (SigV4 verified, no authorization) and `NONE`. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/gateway-inbound-auth.html
- **Offloaded Gateway auth must be backed by another enforcement point.** With `AUTHENTICATE_ONLY` or `NONE`, the gateway enforces nothing, so a policy engine, an interceptor Lambda or the target must authorize; AWS says not to use `NONE` in production without those controls. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/gateway-inbound-auth.html
- **JWT inbound auth on AgentCore Gateway logs the token's subject in CloudTrail.** AWS advises against PII in `sub`. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/gateway-inbound-auth.html
- **AgentCore Gateway outbound auth credential types depend on the target.** `GATEWAY_IAM_ROLE` (SigV4 with the gateway service role) is used for Lambda, API Gateway, Smithy and connector targets; MCP server and OpenAPI targets also need `iamCredentialProvider.service` (for example `bedrock-agentcore`, `execute-api`, `lambda`). The other types are OAuth (via an AgentCore Identity credential provider), API key and none. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/gateway-building-adding-targets-authorization.html ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/gateway-outbound-auth.html
- **Gateway-to-runtime auth types must match.** An IAM runtime needs IAM (SigV4) or Caller IAM credentials, and a JWT runtime needs OAuth (JWT) or Token passthrough; a mismatch returns "Authorization method mismatch". Source: https://repost.aws/knowledge-center/bedrock-resolve-authorization-method
- **MFA is mandatory for the management account root user.** It must be registered at first sign-in or within a 14-day grace period. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/root-user-best-practices.html
- **Centralized root access lets member accounts run without root credentials.** Member-account root credentials can be removed centrally, and privileged tasks run as task-scoped root sessions of up to 15 minutes through `sts:AssumeRoot` from the management or delegated administrator account. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/secure-root-user-access-for-member-accounts-in-aws-organizations/

## 3. Roles and permissions
- **AWS authorization combines several policy types.** They are identity-based IAM policies and resource-based policies, capped by Organizations SCPs (principal-centric) and RCPs (resource-centric), with ABAC through tags and condition keys; an explicit deny always wins. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/iam-policy-types-how-and-when-to-use-them/ ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/runtime-security-best-practices.html
- **The AgentCore Runtime execution role trusts `bedrock-agentcore.amazonaws.com`.** Its trust policy allows `sts:AssumeRole` with `aws:SourceAccount` = your account and `aws:SourceArn` like `arn:aws:bedrock-agentcore:<region>:<account>:*`, for confused-deputy protection. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/runtime-permissions.html
- **The documented AgentCore Runtime execution role permissions cover six areas.**
  - `ecr:BatchGetImage` and `ecr:GetDownloadUrlForLayer` on the repository, plus `ecr:GetAuthorizationToken`.
  - CloudWatch Logs actions on `/aws/bedrock-agentcore/runtimes/*`.
  - `xray:PutTraceSegments`, `xray:PutTelemetryRecords`, `xray:GetSamplingRules` and `xray:GetSamplingTargets`.
  - `cloudwatch:PutMetricData` limited to the `bedrock-agentcore` namespace.
  - `bedrock-agentcore:GetWorkloadAccessToken*` on `workload-identity-directory/default/workload-identity/<agentName>-*`.
  - `bedrock:InvokeModel` and `bedrock:InvokeModelWithResponseStream`.

  Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/runtime-permissions.html
- **Direct code deployment agents use a smaller execution role.** It is the same as the container role without the ECR statements. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/runtime-permissions.html
- **`BedrockAgentCoreFullAccess` is the broad AWS managed policy for building on AgentCore.** It includes `GetWorkloadAccessTokenForUserId`, which issues tokens for arbitrary user ID strings without IdP verification. For production, AWS advises copying only the needed statements, explicitly denying `GetWorkloadAccessTokenForUserId`, and granting `GetWorkloadAccessTokenForJWT`. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/runtime-permissions.html
- **Auto-creating the execution role from the AgentCore console needs three extra IAM permissions.** Console users need `iam:CreateRole` on `role/service-role/AmazonBedrockAgentCoreRuntimeDefaultServiceRole-*`, `iam:CreatePolicy` on `policy/service-role/AmazonBedrockAgentCoreRuntimeExecutionPolicy_*`, and `iam:AttachRolePolicy`. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/runtime-permissions.html
- **The identity that runs `agentcore deploy` needs CDK and CloudFormation permissions.** It needs `sts:GetCallerIdentity`, access to the `CDKToolkit` and `AgentCore-<project>-<target>` stacks, CDK bootstrap or assume-role on the CDK roles, and permissions to create resources and pass roles (S3, ECR, CodeBuild, CloudWatch Logs, AgentCore). AWS says to pre-bootstrap and use least-privilege CDK roles in production. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/runtime-permissions.html
- **`agentcore deploy` also tries to turn on CloudWatch Transaction Search.** That needs `xray:UpdateTraceSegmentDestination` and `logs:PutResourcePolicy`, among others; without those permissions the step is skipped, and `agentcore config transactionSearch false` turns it off. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/runtime-permissions.html
- **Since 2025-10-13, AgentCore workload-identity permissions come from a service-linked role.** For new agents, the service-linked role `AWSServiceRoleForBedrockAgentCoreRuntimeIdentity` (principal `runtime-identity.bedrock-agentcore.amazonaws.com`, policy `BedrockAgentCoreRuntimeIdentityServiceRolePolicy`) replaces manual policies; the deploying identity needs `iam:CreateServiceLinkedRole` for it. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/runtime-oauth.html ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/service-linked-roles.html
- **Other AWS managed policies for AgentCore.** They are:
  - `BedrockAgentCoreNetworkServiceRolePolicy`
  - `AmazonBedrockAgentCoreMemoryBedrockModelInferenceExecutionRolePolicy`
  - `BedrockAgentCoreRuntimeInstancesServiceRolePolicy`
  - `BedrockAgentCoreRuntimeInstancesOperatorRolePolicy`
  - `BedrockAgentCoreRuntimeInstancesInstanceRolePolicy`

  Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/security-iam-awsmanpol.html
- **AgentCore Memory needs an execution role only for custom or override strategies.** For built-in-with-override strategies, AgentCore Memory needs a role passed in `memoryExecutionRoleArn` that trusts `bedrock-agentcore.amazonaws.com` (for example with `AmazonBedrockAgentCoreMemoryBedrockModelInferenceExecutionRolePolicy`); memories without custom strategies ignore the role. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/long-term-configuring-custom-strategies.html ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/cdk/api/v2/docs/aws-cdk-lib.aws_bedrockagentcore-readme.html
- **The AgentCore Gateway service role caps what every caller can do through the gateway.** It is shared by all targets that use `GATEWAY_IAM_ROLE`, so its permissions are the upper bound for every caller. AWS advises minimal scope, separate gateways per trust boundary, and a policy engine on shared gateways. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/gateway-outbound-auth.html
- **AgentCore Policy authorizes agent-to-tool calls through Gateway in Cedar.** Cedar policies are evaluated per tool call over a principal (for example `AgentCore::OAuthUser`), an action (`AgentCore::Action::"<Target>__<tool>"`), a resource (`AgentCore::Gateway`) and request context; the default is deny. Modes are `ENFORCE` and `LOG_ONLY`. AgentCore Policy became GA in March 2026 (preview December 2025). Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/cdk/api/v2/docs/aws-cdk-lib.aws_bedrockagentcore-readme.html ; https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/awsforsap/aws-agentic-standard-operating-procedures-sops-for-erp-automation/ ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/release-notes.html
- **Managing AgentCore Policy takes these IAM actions.**
  - Policy engines and policies: `bedrock-agentcore:CreatePolicyEngine`, `UpdatePolicyEngine`, `CreatePolicy`, `StartPolicyGeneration` and the related Get, List and Delete actions.
  - Attaching to a gateway: `bedrock-agentcore:UpdateGateway`, `ManageResourceScopedPolicy` and `ManageAdminPolicy` on the gateway.

  Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/secure-ai-agents-with-policy-in-amazon-bedrock-agentcore/
- **Cross-account AgentCore Runtime invocation needs two resource-based policies.** `InvokeAgentRuntime` evaluates policies on both the agent runtime and the runtime endpoint, so both need an allow. OAuth-authenticated resources must use `"Principal": "*"` plus conditions such as `aws:SourceVpc` or `aws:SourceVpce`. Cross-account calls count against the resource owner's quotas. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/runtime-security-best-practices.html ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/resource-based-policies.html ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/bedrock-agentcore-limits.html
- **`put-resource-policy` on AgentCore replaces the whole policy.** Multi-tenant updates must resend every statement. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/secure-multi-tenant-ai-agents-with-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-resource-based-policies/
- **An AgentCore Runtime can be restricted to accept calls only from your gateway (July 2026).** IAM runtimes use a resource-based policy with `aws:SourceArn`; JWT runtimes use `allowedWorkloadConfiguration`. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/release-notes.html
- **AgentCore Runtime session context keys let one execution role isolate sessions (October 2026).** With them, sessions that share an execution role can each be limited to their own data; this works on platform version V2. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/release-notes.html
- **AgentCore VPC condition keys can force VPC-connected deployments.** An SCP or IAM policy can deny `bedrock-agentcore:CreateAgentRuntime`, `UpdateAgentRuntime`, `CreateCodeInterpreter` and `CreateBrowser` when `bedrock-agentcore:subnets` or `bedrock-agentcore:securityGroups` is null. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/security-vpc-condition.html
- **AWS managed policies for Amazon Bedrock.** They are:
  - `AmazonBedrockFullAccess`
  - `AmazonBedrockReadOnly`
  - `AmazonBedrockLimitedAccess`
  - `AmazonBedrockMarketplaceAccess`
  - `AmazonBedrockMantleFullAccess`
  - `AmazonBedrockMantleReadOnly`
  - `AmazonBedrockMantleInferenceAccess`

  `AmazonBedrockLimitedAccess` lets Marketplace subscribe actions run only when called through Bedrock (`aws:CalledViaLast`). Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/security-iam-awsmanpol.html
- **Least-privilege Bedrock model access is scoped by resource ARN.** Grant `bedrock:InvokeModel`, `InvokeModelWithResponseStream`, `Converse` and `ConverseStream` on specific `foundation-model` and `inference-profile` ARNs (for example `arn:aws:bedrock:*::foundation-model/anthropic.claude-*` plus `arn:aws:bedrock:*:*:inference-profile/us.anthropic.claude-*`). Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/deploy-ai-agents-on-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-using-github-actions/
- **Bedrock checks several resources for each cross-Region inference call.** For a geographic profile, it authorizes the inference-profile resource, the source-Region model and each candidate destination-Region model. For a global profile, it also authorizes the Region-agnostic `arn:aws:bedrock:::foundation-model/...`, where `aws:RequestedRegion` is `unspecified`. Region-deny SCPs must allow those Regions or use the `bedrock:InferenceProfileArn` exemption. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/geographic-cross-region-inference.html ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/global-cross-region-inference.html
- **`aws-marketplace:Subscribe` can be limited to specific models.** Use the `aws-marketplace:ProductId` condition. A subscription made in one Region makes the model requestable in all Regions where it is offered, even if `Subscribe` is denied there. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-access.html
- **The Bedrock Agents service role needs model, schema, knowledge base and Lambda permissions.** A custom Amazon Bedrock Agents (classic) service role needs model access, S3 read for OpenAPI action-group schemas, knowledge base query and optional guardrail, collaborator, Provisioned Throughput and KMS permissions. Action-group Lambda functions also need a resource-based policy that allows Bedrock to invoke them. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/agents-permissions.html
- **Bedrock Knowledge Bases separate builder permissions from a service role.** Builders need `bedrock:CreateKnowledgeBase`, `StartIngestionJob` and the related actions (or `AmazonBedrockFullAccess`); managed knowledge bases use a service role with model access, data-source access and an optional multimodal storage bucket. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/knowledge-base-prereq-permissions-general.html ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/kb-managed-permissions.html
- **SageMaker AI execution roles trust `sagemaker.amazonaws.com` and are passed with `iam:PassRole`.** The role is passed on `CreateTrainingJob`, `CreateModel`, `CreateDomain`, `CreateProcessingJob` and similar calls; `AmazonSageMakerFullAccess` is the broad managed option. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/sagemaker-roles.html
- **Lambda execution roles must trust `lambda.amazonaws.com`.** Lambda assumes the role on invocation, and function code shouldn't call `sts:AssumeRole` on it. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/lambda/latest/dg/lambda-intro-execution-role.html
- **Step Functions generates least-privilege state machine roles in the console.** When you create a state machine in the console, it builds a Region-scoped role for Bedrock integrations (`InvokeModel`, `CreateModelCustomizationJob`). Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/step-functions/latest/dg/connect-bedrock.html
- **EventBridge rule target roles trust `events.amazonaws.com`.** EventBridge Scheduler roles trust `scheduler.amazonaws.com` and should carry confused-deputy conditions. Source: https://repost.aws/knowledge-center/eventbridge-cross-account-targets ; https://repost.aws/knowledge-center/troubleshoot-eventbridge-scheduler-errors

## 4. Governance, security and compliance
- **Bedrock has PrivateLink interface endpoints.** Services include `com.amazonaws.<region>.bedrock-runtime` and Bedrock Mantle endpoints; endpoint policies can limit actions (for example `bedrock:InvokeModel`, `bedrock-mantle:CreateInference`) and principals. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/vpc-interface-endpoints.html ; https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/use-aws-privatelink-to-set-up-private-access-to-amazon-bedrock/
- **IP-based conditions on Bedrock fail through VPC endpoints.** `aws:SourceIp` isn't present when a request goes through a VPC endpoint, and network context is redacted when an AWS service calls Bedrock for you, so `IpAddress` conditions can't be met in those paths. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-access.html
- **AgentCore supports PrivateLink.** The Gateway data plane endpoint is `com.amazonaws.<region>.bedrock-agentcore.gateway`. Control-plane PrivateLink for Runtime, Memory, Gateway, built-in tools and Evaluations arrived in March 2026, and VPC egress for Identity, Gateway and Runtime in April 2026. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/vpc-interface-endpoints.html ; https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/secure-ingress-connectivity-to-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-gateway-using-interface-vpc-endpoints/ ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/release-notes.html
- **RCPs cap what resources allow across the organization.** They are typically used for data perimeters with `aws:PrincipalOrgID`, `aws:SourceOrgID`, `aws:SourceVpc` and `aws:PrincipalIsAWSService`; like SCPs, they don't apply to service-linked roles. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/whitepapers/latest/building-a-data-perimeter-on-aws/perimeter-overview.html
- **Bedrock stores customer content at rest in the Region where you use it.** Prompts and outputs aren't shared with model providers. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/faqs/
- **Bedrock cross-Region inference changes where processing happens.** Geographic profiles keep processing within a geography (for example US, EU, APAC, Australia or Japan), while global profiles route to any supported commercial Region; an SCP can block global profiles with `aws:RequestedRegion` `unspecified` plus `bedrock:InferenceProfileArn` like `arn:aws:bedrock:*:*:inference-profile/global.*`. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/geographic-cross-region-inference.html ; https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/securing-amazon-bedrock-cross-region-inference-geographic-and-global/ ; https://repost.aws/knowledge-center/bedrock-service-unavailable-error
- **Bedrock doesn't train models on customer data.** AWS and third-party providers don't use Bedrock inputs or outputs to train Amazon Nova, Amazon Titan or third-party models, Bedrock doesn't store or review prompts and completions, and there's nothing to opt out of. Source: https://repost.aws/knowledge-center/amazon-bedrock-model-data-use ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ai/responsible-ai/nova-2-lite/overview.html
- **Amazon Q Developer Free tier may use content for service improvement; Pro and Amazon Q Business don't.** You opt out with an AWS Organizations AI services opt-out policy for the console, Slack and Teams, and in IDE settings for the IDE. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/q/developer/faqs/ ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/organizations/latest/userguide/orgs_manage_policies_ai-opt-out.html
- **AWS Quick says it doesn't train models on customer data.** Its stated attestations are HIPAA eligibility, FedRAMP, SOC 2 and ISO 27001. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/11/amazon-quick-aws-quick/
- **A specific Bedrock guardrail can be required by IAM or SCP.** The `bedrock:GuardrailIdentifier` condition key (added March 2025) works on `InvokeModel`, `InvokeModelWithResponseStream`, `Converse` and `ConverseStream`; an SCP with `Null` `bedrock:GuardrailIdentifier` = `true` denies calls that have no guardrail. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-permissions-id.html ; https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/implementing-least-privilege-access-for-amazon-bedrock/
- **Bedrock Guardrails can be enforced at organization and account level.** A management-account Amazon Bedrock policy in AWS Organizations applies a guardrail to every model call in OUs or accounts, and an account-level default guardrail is also supported; when an application guardrail also applies, the union applies and the most restrictive control wins. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-enforcements.html ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/help-panel/bedrock/latest/console/hp-guardrails-org-level-management.html
- **Org-level enforcement of a Standard-tier guardrail needs resource-based policies.** Both the guardrail and its guardrail profile (one per destination Region) must allow the organization, or calls fail with "The provided resource ARN is from a different account". Source: https://repost.aws/knowledge-center/bedrock-troubleshoot-guardrail-error
- **AgentCore Policy supports Bedrock Guardrails (July 2026).** Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/release-notes.html
- **Bedrock model invocation logging is off by default.** When turned on, it writes full requests, responses and metadata to CloudWatch Logs or S3 in the same account and Region for `Converse`, `ConverseStream`, `InvokeModel` and `InvokeModelWithResponseStream` on `bedrock-runtime` only; calls through `bedrock-mantle` aren't captured. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-invocation-logging.html
- **Bedrock CloudTrail coverage splits into management and data events.** `InvokeModel`, `Converse` and their streaming variants are management events. `InvokeAgent` (`AWS::Bedrock::AgentAlias`), `Retrieve` and `RetrieveAndGenerate` (`AWS::Bedrock::KnowledgeBase`), `InvokeFlow` and `ApplyGuardrail` are data events that need advanced event selectors. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/logging-using-cloudtrail.html
- **AgentCore Runtime data-plane calls are CloudTrail data events.** `InvokeAgentRuntime`, `InvokeAgentRuntimeCommand`, `InvokeAgentRuntimeCommandShell` and `InvokeAgentRuntimeProxy` use resource type `AWS::BedrockAgentCore::Runtime`; they aren't logged by default, and data events cost extra. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/runtime-session-endpoints.html ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/harness-operations.html
- **AgentCore compliance attestations.** AgentCore holds ISO and CSA STAR certifications (February 2026) and is in scope for SOC 1, 2 and 3 (July 2026); it has been GA in AWS GovCloud (US-West) since May 2026. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/release-notes.html
- **Bedrock compliance attestations.** Bedrock is in scope for:
  - FedRAMP Moderate
  - SOC 1, 2 and 3 (since 2023-08-15)
  - ISO 9001, 27001, 27017, 27018, 27701, 22301 and 20000
  - CSA STAR Level 2
  - HIPAA eligibility and GDPR use

  Source: https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/faqs/ ; https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/alps/security_bedrock/

### Quotas that throttle agents
- **Bedrock inference is throttled on tokens per minute and requests per minute, per model and source Region.** Embedding models are throttled on RPM only. `bedrock-runtime` and `bedrock-mantle` have separate quota pools for the same model, and defaults can be lower than published based on account age and history. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/quotas.html
- **Bedrock counts output tokens at a burndown rate against TPM.** Output tokens are weighted by the model's rate, for example 5:1 for Claude Opus 4.6, Sonnet 4.6 and Sonnet 4.5 and 1:1 for Claude Haiku 4.5 and Nova, so quota used = input + cache-write + output × rate. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/run-generative-ai-inference-with-amazon-bedrock-in-asia-pacific-new-zealand/
- **Bedrock cross-Region quotas are separate.** They appear as "Cross-Region InvokeModel tokens per minute" per model in the source Region, and US geographic and global profiles draw from different pools. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/access-openai-gpt-models-on-amazon-bedrock-from-canada-with-cross-region-inference/
- **AgentCore Runtime data-plane calls share 1,000 TPS per account.** `InvokeAgentRuntime` and the other data-plane APIs share one adjustable quota, and new sessions are limited to 25 TPS per account across all endpoints (consolidated in August 2026). This replaces the 200 TPS per agent and 400 TPM per endpoint stated in the July 2026 notes. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/bedrock-agentcore-limits.html ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/release-notes.html
- **AgentCore Runtime session and invocation limits.**
  - Active sessions: 5,000 per account in us-east-1 and us-west-2, 2,500 elsewhere (adjustable).
  - Agents: 1,000 per account.
  - Synchronous request timeout: 15 minutes.
  - Payload: 100 MB.
  - Streaming duration: 60 minutes.
  - Idle session timeout: 15 minutes by default.
  - Maximum session: 8 hours on microVMs; up to 14 days for async jobs on Instances.

  Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/bedrock-agentcore-limits.html
- **AgentCore Gateway default limits (adjustable).**
  - Tool-call and tool-list rate: 200 TPS per gateway and per account.
  - Concurrent connections: 5,000.
  - Search-based tool calls: 25 TPM.
  - Payload: 6 MB.
  - Targets: 100 per gateway.
  - Invocation timeout: 15 minutes.

  Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/bedrock-agentcore-limits.html
- **AgentCore Gateway owners can set their own rate limits (August 2026).** Limits are scoped by JWT `sub`, IAM principal, target, tool or model, and measured in RPS/RPM, TPM or connections. The effective rate is `min(service_managed, customer_configured)`, and `rate=0` blocks a caller. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/release-notes.html
- **AgentCore Identity rate limits (all adjustable).** `GetWorkloadAccessToken*`, `GetResourceOauth2Token` and `GetResourceApiKey` allow 200 TPS per account, `CompleteResourceTokenAuth` 100 TPS, and credential-provider CRUD 20 TPS. Limits are 50 OAuth2 and 50 API-key credential providers and 11,000 workload identities per account per Region. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/bedrock-agentcore-limits.html
- **AgentCore Policy limits (not adjustable).** Each account and Region can have 1,000 policy engines, with 1,000 policies each; the Cedar schema is capped at 400 KB per engine; `CreatePolicy` allows 5 TPS. Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/bedrock-agentcore-limits.html
- **Lambda account concurrency defaults to 1,000 per Region and can be raised.** Throttled synchronous calls return HTTP 429. (secondary: AWS Compute Blog) Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/compute/understanding-aws-lambda-scaling-and-throughput/
