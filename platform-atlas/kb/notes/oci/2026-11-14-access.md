# Oracle Cloud Infrastructure: access, identity, roles, governance and commercial model (as of 2026-11-14)

## 1. Getting access
- **The tenancy is the root compartment.** An OCI tenancy is the root compartment that holds all of an organization's OCI resources; Oracle creates it at sign-up, and users, groups, dynamic groups and federation providers are managed at tenancy level. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Identity/Concepts/overview.htm
- **Compartments can nest six levels deep.** OCI compartments are logical (not physical) groupings for access control, billing and isolation; subcompartments can form hierarchies six levels deep, and a child inherits policies from its parents. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/Content/Identity/Tasks/managingcompartments.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Identity/Concepts/overview.htm
- **IAM changes happen in the home region.** IAM resources are global, but their master definitions live in the tenancy's home region; IAM changes must be made there and propagate to other regions. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Identity/Concepts/overview.htm
- **A tenancy lives in exactly one realm.** Regions are grouped into realms, and a tenancy can access only regions in its realm: commercial `OC1` (45 commercial regions listed), plus separate government realms (US Government Cloud, US Defense Cloud, UK Sovereign Cloud, Australia Government and Defence) and dedicated realms. The Generative AI region page also names `OC2` (US Gov), `OC3` (US DoD), `OC4` (UK Gov) and `OC19` (EU Sovereign). Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/General/Concepts/regions.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/model-endpoint-regions.htm
- **Region subscription depends on the contract type.** Trial, Free Tier and Pay As You Go tenancies can subscribe to one region (PAYG can request more through a `Subscribed region count` limit increase). Universal Credit tenancies can subscribe to all public commercial regions. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/General/Concepts/regions.htm
- **Enterprises buy OCI through Universal Credits contracts or Pay As You Go.** Oracle's billing models are Annual Universal Credits, Monthly Universal Credit (subject to Oracle approval), Universal Credits Pay As You Go, BYOL, Oracle Cloud at Customer and Government Subscriptions. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/get-started/subscriptions-cloud/ocpib/billing-models-offered.html
- **The self-service entry is the Oracle Cloud Free Tier.** It combines Always Free services with a 30-day trial of US$300 in credits (select countries). A credit card is required, and only one free account is allowed per person. (secondary: search summary of the Oracle page) Source: https://www.oracle.com/cloud/free/
- **Oracle Database@AWS is bought through an AWS Marketplace private offer (or a public offer).** You request a private offer from the AWS console, Oracle sales negotiates it, you accept it in AWS Marketplace, and onboarding links the AWS account to a new or existing OCI tenancy. Autonomous Database Serverless also has a public offer. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-aws/oaaws-task-2-request-offer.htm ; https://docs.aws.amazon.com/odb/latest/UserGuide/setting-up.html
- **Oracle Database@Azure is bought through an Azure Marketplace private offer.** You accept the offer in Azure Marketplace under Private Offer Management, then link or create an OCI tenancy. Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/oracle/oracle-db/onboard-oracle-database
- **Multicloud onboarding creates OCI artifacts you must not change.** Oracle Database@AWS onboarding creates the base compartment `MulticloudLink_AWS_<YYYYMMDDHHMMSS>` and the policies `MulticloudLink_AWS_Management`, `MulticloudLink_AWS_<UNIQUE_ID>_User_Group_Policies`, `..._Observability` and `..._Tenant_Level`. The docs say not to change or delete them. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/Content/database-at-aws/oaaws-rbac.htm
- **Day-one asks for a client engagement.** Ask for: (1) the tenancy OCID, home region and subscribed regions; (2) a user in the right identity domain, with that domain's name; (3) membership in a group with a compartment-scoped policy (for example `manage generative-ai-family` and `manage genai-agent-family` in the project compartment); (4) a sandbox compartment OCID; (5) the dynamic groups or resource-principal policies your agent runtime needs; (6) VCN, subnet and private endpoint details if public endpoints are blocked. This checklist is derived from the policy pages cited in sections 2 and 3. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/iam-policies.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai-agents/iam-policies.htm

## 2. Identity and authentication
- **OCI IAM uses identity domains.** Each tenancy has a `Default` identity domain in the root compartment. Identity domains provide authentication, SSO and identity lifecycle for OCI, Oracle SaaS and non-Oracle apps; the domain types you can create depend on your subscription. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Identity/home.htm
- **There are six identity domain types.** They are `Free` (2,000 users, 2 non-Oracle apps), `Oracle Apps` (1,000,000 users, 2 non-Oracle apps), `Oracle Apps Premium` (hybrid IAM for Oracle enterprise apps), `Premium` (full feature set, 5,000 non-Oracle apps, App Gateway for any app), `External User` (consumer/non-employee, billed per stored user) and `External Active User` (billed per monthly active user). External domains have no dynamic groups and no OCI resource management, and the Default domain can't be changed to an External type. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Identity/sku/overview.htm
- **Human SSO uses SAML 2.0 or OIDC federation into an identity domain.** You can add SAML 2.0 identity providers and export the domain's SAML metadata, OIDC is supported for compliant applications, and Apple became a supported social IdP on 2025-11-05. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Identity/home.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/services/identity/
- **Console MFA is on by default for new tenancies.** The seeded sign-on policy `Security Policy for OCI Console` has rules for MFA for administrators and for all users. It is active by default in new tenancies and is not auto-activated if the default sign-on policy was modified or the tenancy is federated. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Security/Reference/iam_security_topic-iam_mfa_with_identity_domains.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/Content/Identity/signonpolicies/managingsignonpolicies.htm
- **Users have per-credential limits.** Per user: 3 API signing keys, 2 auth tokens (auth tokens do not expire), 2 Customer Secret keys (S3 compatibility), 2 SMTP credentials, and 10 OAuth 2.0 client credentials (up to 10 scopes each; the limit can be raised). There are also IAM database passwords. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/Content/Identity/Tasks/managingcredentials.htm
- **API signing keys are the default SDK and CLI credential.** The SDK config file (`~/.oci/config`) holds `user`, `fingerprint`, `tenancy`, `region` and `key_file`. Create it with `oci setup config`, or with `oci setup bootstrap` for a browser-based flow. Oracle advises using it only where storing private keys locally is acceptable. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/API/Concepts/sdk_authentication_methods.htm
- **Session tokens last 60 minutes.** `oci session authenticate` creates a browser-based session token that expires after 60 minutes by default. It is not supported in the PowerShell or Ruby SDKs. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/API/Concepts/sdk_authentication_methods.htm
- **Auth tokens are for clients that can't sign requests.** Auth tokens are Oracle-generated strings for third-party APIs that don't support OCI request signing (for example Swift or OCIR docker login). They never expire and can't be chosen or changed. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/Content/Identity/Tasks/managingcredentials.htm
- **OAuth 2.0 client credentials return 1-hour tokens.** IAM user OAuth 2.0 client credentials call `https://auth.<oci_region>.oraclecloud.com/oauth2/token` with `grant_type=client_credentials&scope=<audience>-<scope>`. Access tokens expire after 3600 seconds, and regenerating the secret invalidates the old one. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/Content/Identity/Tasks/managingcredentials.htm
- **Instance principals let compute instances call OCI without stored credentials.** OCI issues instance certificates and rotates them several times a day; instances are granted access through dynamic groups. Use `oci ... --auth instance_principal` or `OCI_CLI_AUTH=instance_principal`, Python `oci.auth.signers.InstancePrincipalsSecurityTokenSigner()`, or Terraform `auth = "InstancePrincipal"`. Every instance principal gets `compartment_inspect`, which can't be revoked. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Identity/Tasks/callingservicesfrominstances.htm
- **Resource principals cover non-instance resources.** OCI Functions, Generative AI Agents, data ingestion jobs and hosted applications authenticate with a Resource Principal Session Token (RPST), a JWT with `res_tenant` and `res_compartment` claims. For Functions the token is cached for 15 minutes, so policy changes can take that long to apply. Python uses `oci.auth.signers.get_resource_principals_signer()`; the env vars are `OCI_RESOURCE_PRINCIPAL_RPST` and `OCI_RESOURCE_PRINCIPAL_PRIVATE_PEM`. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Functions/Tasks/functionsaccessingociresources.htm
- **OKE Workload Identity requires enhanced clusters.** OCI Kubernetes Engine workload identity works only on enhanced clusters and only through an OCI SDK (not the Console or API), and it cannot be used with dynamic groups. Minimum SDKs: Go v65.32.0, Java v2.93.0 / v3.88.0, Python v2.111.0, .NET v87.3.0, Ruby v2.19.0. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/ContEng/Tasks/contenggrantingworkloadaccesstoresources.htm
- **OKE Workload Identity policy syntax.** `Allow any-user to <verb> <resource> in <location> where all {request.principal.type = 'workload', request.principal.namespace = '<ns>', request.principal.service_account = '<sa>', request.principal.cluster_id = '<cluster-ocid>'}`. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/ContEng/Tasks/contenggrantingworkloadaccesstoresources.htm
- **External JWTs can be exchanged for a UPST.** Since 2025-07-17, a JWT from an external IdP can be exchanged for a short-lived User Principal Session Token. The exchange is a POST to `https://<domainURL>/oauth2/v1/token` with `grant_type=urn:ietf:params:oauth:grant-type:token-exchange`, `requested_token_type=urn:oci:token-type:oci-upst`, `subject_token_type=jwt` and `public_key`, and needs an Identity Propagation Trust (`/admin/v1/IdentityPropagationTrusts`) plus a confidential app's client credentials. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Identity/api-getstarted/json_web_token_exchange.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/identity/identity-jwt-to-upst-token-exchange.htm
- **The UPST exchange can impersonate a service user.** With `allowImpersonation: true`, the UPST is issued for a mapped identity-domain service user, which has no password or API keys, and the original subject is recorded in `source_authn_prin`. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Identity/api-getstarted/json_web_token_exchange.htm
- **JWT-to-RPST exchange is keyless workload identity federation.** IAM released JWT-to-RPST token exchange for workload identity federation on 2026-08-26. External JWTs (GitHub Actions, Kubernetes, other clouds) are exchanged with `requested_token_type=urn:oci:token-type:oci-rpst` for ephemeral RPSTs; `claimPropagations` copies selected claims, prefixed `ext_`, into the token for policy evaluation. Oracle's blog frames this as "zero-footprint access for external workloads and AI agents". The claim details come from Oracle blog and A-Team posts. (secondary for claim details) Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/services/identity/ ; https://blogs.oracle.com/cloud-infrastructure/wif-with-ephemeral-rpst ; https://www.ateam-oracle.com/github-actions-oci-a-guide-to-secure-oidc-token-exchange
- **Identity domains support ID-JAG cross-domain token exchange (2026-10-09).** OCI IAM cross-app access exchanges a user ID token for an Identity Assertion JWT Authorization Grant (`urn:ietf:params:oauth:token-type:id-jag`) and redeems it at a target identity domain. It also accepts ID-JAGs from trusted external IdPs such as Okta. (secondary for details) Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/services/identity/ ; https://blogs.oracle.com/cloud-infrastructure/?p=18791
- **Recent identity domain OAuth additions.** OAuth resource indicators (2026-10-07) and Client ID Metadata Document support for OAuth clients (2026-09-23). Since 2026-04-23, client secrets of Oracle-provisioned applications are no longer visible. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/services/identity/
- **OCI Generative AI API keys are a separate service credential.** They are bearer secrets created in the Generative AI service (not IAM signing keys). Each key has up to two secrets with their own expirations, shown only once, and can be set active, inactive or revoked with `SetApiKeyState`. The default limit is 100 per tenancy (limit name `api-key-count`). Callers send `Authorization: Bearer <secret>` to `https://inference.generativeai.<region>.oci.oraclecloud.com/20231130/actions/v1/chat/completions` or `.../responses`. Launched 2026-01-21. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/api-keys.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/services/generative-ai/
- **Oracle recommends IAM authentication over API keys for agents running on OCI.** Generative AI API keys are positioned for testing and early development. OCI IAM signed requests are recommended for production, for workloads on OCI Functions or OKE, and to avoid long-lived keys. The `oci-genai-auth` package provides `OciSessionAuth`, `OciUserPrincipalAuth`, `OciInstancePrincipalAuth` and `OciResourcePrincipalAuth` as httpx auth for the OpenAI SDK (`api_key="not-used"`, base URL `https://inference.generativeai.<region>.oci.oraclecloud.com/openai/v1`, `project=<generativeaiproject OCID>`). Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/get-started-agents.htm
- **Hosted agentic applications support four inbound auth modes.** OCI Generative AI hosted applications accept identity domain bearer tokens (an OAuth confidential app with the client credentials grant, primary audience and scopes), identity domain browser sessions (OAuth 2.1 Authorization Code with PKCE; GA 2026-10-07), OCI IAM request signatures (2026-07-15), or "No authentication" (2026-10-21; the app must enforce its own access control). Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/governance.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/app-authentication.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/services/generative-ai/
- **One docs page says OAuth is the only auth type for agentic tasks.** The Generative AI "Permissions to Generative AI Resources" page says "OAuth is the only supported authentication type for agentic tasks". The newer get-started-agents page documents both API keys and IAM signing for the Responses API, so the OAuth-only statement likely applies to hosted applications. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/access.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/get-started-agents.htm
- **Autonomous Database accepts IAM database tokens.** Enable IAM auth with `DBMS_CLOUD_ADMIN.ENABLE_EXTERNAL_AUTHENTICATION(type => 'OCI_IAM')` (add `force => TRUE` if another external auth is enabled). Clients get tokens with `oci iam db-token get` (also `--auth security_token` or `--auth instance_principal`), need TCPS, `SSL_SERVER_DN_MATCH=ON` and `TOKEN_AUTH=OCI_TOKEN`, and 19c clients must be at least 19.16. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/autonomous-database-serverless/doc/enable-iam-authentication.html ; https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/autonomous-database-serverless/doc/iam-access-database.html
- **There is no dedicated "agent identity" principal type.** Agents use existing principal types. A Generative AI Agents agent acts as a resource principal (`request.principal.type='genaiagent'`, dynamic group `resource.type='genaiagent'`), and Generative AI API keys appear as `request.principal.type='generativeaiapikey'`. The IAM release notes for 2025-11 to 2026-11 announce no agent-specific identity type. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai-agents/iam-policies.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/services/identity/

## 3. Roles and permissions
- **OCI IAM policy grammar.** `Allow <subject> to <verb> <resource-type> in <tenancy | compartment <name>> [where <conditions>]`. Subjects are `group`, `dynamic-group`, `any-user` or `any-group`, and the verbs are cumulative: `inspect` < `read` < `use` < `manage`. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/Content/Identity/policieshow/Policy_Basics.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai-agents/iam-policies.htm
- **Groups outside the Default domain must be domain-qualified.** Write `group <identity_domain_name>/<group_name>`, or with quotes for dynamic groups, for example `dynamic-group '<idcs-domain-name>/<dynamic-group-name>'`. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/Content/Identity/policieshow/Policy_Basics.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai-agents/iam-policies.htm
- **The Administrators group has a fixed policy.** `Allow group Administrators to manage all-resources in tenancy` can't be changed or deleted, and the group must always have at least one member. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Identity/Concepts/overview.htm
- **IAM deny policies are opt-in and irreversible (GA 2025-11-20).** The syntax is `deny <subject> to <verb> <resource> in <scope> [where ...]` (also `deny admit` and `deny endorse`), and deny overrides allow. Only the default domain's Administrators group can enable deny policies, and that group is always exempt. Enabling creates a root policy restricting who manages deny statements, and the feature can't be turned off. The Policy Basics page still says "Policies only allow access", which predates this feature. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/Content/Identity/policysyntax/denypolicies.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/identity/deny-policies.htm
- **Cross-tenancy access uses paired endorse and admit statements.** For example, an `admit` in the resource tenancy granting `use database-connections` must match an `endorse` in the user's tenancy. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/autonomous-database-serverless/doc/iam-access-database-cross-tenancy.html
- **Identity domain administrator roles.** The roles are Identity Domain Administrator (superuser for the domain), Security Administrator, Application Administrator, User Administrator, User Manager, Help Desk Administrator and Audit Administrator. Assigning them inside a domain is separate from OCI IAM policies. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Identity/roles/understand-administrator-roles.htm
- **Identity Domain Administrator is the role required for trusts and federation.** Managing Identity Propagation Trusts needs a token from an app holding that role. Multicloud federation setup also names the OCI Identity Domain Administrator as the OCI-side persona. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Identity/api-getstarted/json_web_token_exchange.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-gcp/oagcp-prerequisites.htm
- **The Generative AI aggregate resource type is `generative-ai-family`.** Grant it with `allow group <group> to manage generative-ai-family in compartment <compartment>` (or `in tenancy`). Oracle recommends the full family only for admins or sandbox users. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/iam-policies.htm
- **Individual Generative AI resource types.** `generative-ai-chat`, `generative-ai-text-embedding`, `generative-ai-text-rerank`, `generative-ai-model`, `generative-ai-imported-model`, `generative-ai-dedicated-ai-cluster`, `generative-ai-endpoint`, `generative-ai-private-endpoint`, `generative-ai-routing-profile`, `generative-ai-apikey`, `generative-ai-project`, `generative-ai-sandbox`, `generative-ai-vector-store`, `generative-ai-vectorstore-connector`, `generative-ai-vectorstore-file`, `generative-ai-file`, `generative-ai-hosted-application`, `generative-ai-hosted-application-iam`, `generative-ai-hosted-deployment`, `generative-ai-container`, `generative-ai-work-request`. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/iam-policies.htm
- **Additional Generative AI resource types appear on other pages.** `generative-ai-response`, `generative-ai-semantic-store` and `generative-ai-nl2sql` are used in the agents quick start but are not in the `generative-ai-family` list on the IAM page. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/get-started-agents.htm
- **Least-privilege inference for a human or app group.** Use `allow group <group> to use generative-ai-chat in compartment <c>`. Since 2026-09-03 you can limit it to specific models with `where target.model.id in ('<model-1>','<model-2>')`. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/add-api-permission.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/services/generative-ai/
- **Generative AI API keys need an IAM policy keyed on the key principal.** Create the key, then add a policy such as `allow any-user to use generative-ai-family in compartment <c> where ALL {request.principal.type='generativeaiapikey', request.principal.id='<api-key-OCID>'}`. Narrower forms use `generative-ai-chat` with `target.model.id in (...)`, or `manage generative-ai-response` for the Responses API. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/add-api-permission.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/get-started-agents.htm
- **The Generative AI Agents aggregate is `genai-agent-family`.** Grant it with `allow group <genai-agent-administrators> to manage genai-agent-family in compartment <c>`. It covers `genai-agent`, `genai-agent-knowledge-base`, `genai-agent-data-source`, `genai-agent-data-ingestion-job`, `genai-agent-endpoint`, `genai-agent-work-request`, `genai-agent-session` and `genai-agent-tool`. The page says "seven" but lists eight. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai-agents/iam-policies.htm
- **A Generative AI Agents agent needs permission to call models.** Coarse form: `allow any-user to use generative-ai-family in compartment <c> where any {request.principal.type='genaiagent'}`. Fine-grained form: `use generative-ai-chat` + `read generative-ai-model` (plus `read generative-ai-endpoint` for dedicated endpoints) with the same condition. Dynamic-group form: matching rule `ALL {resource.type='genaiagent'}` with `allow dynamic-group <dg> to use generative-ai-chat in compartment <c>`. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai-agents/iam-policies.htm
- **Agents data ingestion reads Object Storage through a resource principal.** Matching rule `ALL {resource.type='genaiagentdataingestionjob'}`, optionally with `resource.compartment.id`. Policy: `allow dynamic-group <dg> to read objects in compartment <c>`. Bucket, compartment and tag conditions work; `target.object.name` or prefix conditions are not supported and can fail ingestion. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai-agents/iam-policies.htm
- **Agents SQL and database tools read Vault secrets through the agent resource principal.** With dynamic group `ALL {resource.type='genaiagent'}`, grant `allow dynamic-group <dg> to read secret-bundle in compartment <c>` and `allow dynamic-group <dg> to read database-tools-family in compartment <c>`. Domain-qualify the group name outside the Default domain. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai-agents/iam-policies.htm
- **Hosted agent deployments run as a resource principal.** Matching rule: `any {resource.type='generativeaihostedapplication', resource.type='generativeaihostedapplicationiam', resource.type='generativeaihosteddeployment'}`. Dynamic group policies: `read repos` (OCIR), `read vss-family`, and optionally `read object-family`. Users get `use` or `manage generative-ai-hosted-application` and `generativeaihosteddeployment`. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/deploy-permissions.htm
- **NL2SQL semantic stores run as a resource principal.** Matching rule `all {resource.type='generativeaisemanticstore'}`, granted `use database-tools-family`, `read secret-family`, `read database-family`, `read autonomous-database-family` and `use generative-ai-family`. Admins get `manage generative-ai-semantic-store` and `manage generative-ai-nl2sql`. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/get-started-agents.htm
- **Functions resource principals use dynamic groups.** Matching rule: `ALL {resource.type = 'fnfunc', resource.compartment.id = '<ocid>'}`; free-form tags are not supported in matching rules. Example policy: `allow dynamic-group <dg> to manage objects in compartment <c> where all {target.bucket.name='<bucket>'}`. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Functions/Tasks/functionsaccessingociresources.htm
- **Autonomous Database IAM token access needs a policy.** `Allow group DBUsers to use autonomous-database-family in compartment <c>`, optionally `where target.id = '<adb-ocid>'`. The policy is needed for IAM tokens but not for IAM database passwords. Instance and resource principals must be mapped through a dynamic group. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/autonomous-database-serverless/doc/iam-create-groups-policies.html
- **Vault resource types.** Individual types are `vaults`, `keys`, `key-delegate`, `hsm-cluster`, `secrets`, `secret-versions` and `secret-bundles`. The aggregate `secret-family` covers secrets, secret-versions and secret-bundles. `key-delegate` lets integrated services use a key without letting the grantee use it directly, and reading secret content needs `read secret-bundles` (`SECRET_BUNDLE_READ`). Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Identity/Reference/keypolicyreference.htm
- **Oracle Database@AWS uses predefined OCI groups.** They include `aws-db-family-administrators`, `aws-db-family-readers`, `aws-network-administrators`, `aws-exa-infra-administrators`, `aws-exadb-vm-cluster-administrators`, `aws-exa-cdb-administrators`, `aws-exa-pdb-administrators`, `aws-autonomous-vm-cluster-administrators`, `aws-costmgmt-administrators`, `aws-metrics-readers` and `aws-dbmgmt-administrators`. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/Content/database-at-aws/oaaws-rbac.htm
- **Oracle Database@Azure maps Entra ID groups to Azure roles and replicated OCI groups.** For example, `odbaa-exa-infra-administrators` maps to the Azure role `Oracle.Database Exadata Infrastructure Administrator` and is replicated to OCI during optional identity federation. Entra ID Administrator (Azure) and Identity Domain Administrator (OCI) do the setup. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-azure/oaaonboard-task-7.htm ; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/oracle/oracle-db/oracle-database-groups-roles
- **Oracle Database@Google Cloud uses predefined Google Cloud IAM roles.** Examples are `roles/oracledatabase.exadbVmClusterAdmin` and `roles/oracledatabase.odbSubnetUser`, granted at project level. Federation to OCI is optional and needs an OCI Identity Domain Administrator. Users holding the Identity Domain Administrator role manage EPM users, SSO and role assignment in the OCI Console, and IdP groups (for example Entra ID) can be synced. EPM itself is covered in a separate note. ## 4. Governance, security and compliance
- **The Generative AI service doesn't retain prompts or responses.** OCI Generative AI does not retain prompts and responses during inference, and model providers don't use them for training. Oracle has contractual Zero Data Retention agreements with Google (Gemini) and xAI (Grok). Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/data-handling.htm
- **Fine-tuning data is used only for your custom model.** Training data is deleted from the fine-tuning cluster when the job completes, is not shared with third-party providers, and is not used to improve general models. It is AES-256 encrypted, optionally also with customer-managed Vault keys, and data in transit uses TLS 1.2. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/data-handling.htm
- **Dedicated AI clusters are single-tenant.** Prompts on a dedicated AI cluster are processed by your model on that cluster, not sent to an external provider, and the cluster isn't shared with other customers. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/data-handling.htm
- **Some models send data outside OCI.** Cohere, Meta Llama and OpenAI gpt-oss requests stay in OCI. Gemini and Grok requests go to Google or xAI locations: Gemini in US regions uses Google Americas, Frankfurt uses Google EU, and Osaka or Hyderabad may process globally. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/data-handling.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/model-endpoint-regions.htm
- **Generative AI Agents doesn't train on customer data.** OCI Generative AI Agents does not use knowledge bases or chat data for training and does not retain chat data beyond the session. Managed knowledge base data is stored isolated per customer. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai-agents/data-handling.htm
- **Generative AI regions.** OCI Generative AI regions include us-chicago-1, us-ashburn-1, us-phoenix-1, eu-frankfurt-1, uk-london-1, sa-saopaulo-1, ap-osaka-1, ap-hyderabad-1, me-riyadh-1, me-dubai-1 and me-abudhabi-1, plus US Gov West, US DoD West, UK Gov South and EU Sovereign Central (dedicated only or mostly dedicated). Sydney has no local processing and uses routing profiles. Availability is per model and per mode. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/model-endpoint-regions.htm
- **Cross-region routing profiles extend on-demand access.** Since 2026-10-20, routing profiles give on-demand access across 30 regions by sending requests to another region, so processing may happen outside the calling region. On-demand access was added in Jeddah, Jerusalem and Marseille on 2026-10-30. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/services/generative-ai/ ; https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/model-endpoint-regions.htm
- **Generative AI Agents regions.** OCI Generative AI Agents is available in us-ashburn-1, us-chicago-1, us-phoenix-1, sa-saopaulo-1, eu-frankfurt-1, uk-london-1 and ap-osaka-1. Enterprise AI Agents (Responses API) launched in Chicago, Ashburn, Phoenix, Frankfurt, London, Osaka, Hyderabad, Sao Paulo and Riyadh. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai-agents/overview.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/generative-ai/enterprise-ai.htm
- **Generative AI private endpoints keep model traffic in a VCN.** They are available for dedicated endpoints, for on-demand mode (2026-01-21) and for imported models (2026-06-30). You need a VCN, a private subnet and a DNS prefix; the default limit is 5 private endpoints per tenancy. Zero Trust Packet Routing (ZPR) security attributes can be added (2026-03-27). Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/create-private-endpoint.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/services/generative-ai/
- **A service gateway gives private access to Oracle services in the same region.** VCN resources in private subnets reach Oracle Services Network services (for example Object Storage) without an internet or NAT gateway, using the CIDR labels `All <region> Services in Oracle Services Network` or `OCI <region> Object Storage`. IAM network sources can restrict bucket access to a specific VCN. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Network/Tasks/servicegateway.htm
- **Autonomous Database has three network access options.** They are secure access from everywhere (public, with optional ACL), access from allowed IPs and VCNs only (ACL required), and private endpoint only (VCN with NSG rules). Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/autonomous-database-serverless/doc/network-access-options.html
- **Generative AI Guardrails screen inputs and outputs.** They provide content moderation, image moderation (v1.1.0, 2026-05-29), prompt injection detection, PII detection and topic management (v1.2.0). On-demand models use the `ApplyGuardrails` API; dedicated endpoints use Inform or Block mode. Versions released: 1.0.0 on 2026-02-26, 1.0.1 on 2026-05-26; pin one with `guardrailVersionConfig`. Topic management and PII detection are English only. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/guardrails.htm
- **The OCI Audit service records API calls.** It keeps logs for 365 days, a tenancy-wide setting; export to Object Storage for longer retention. The principal is recorded, for example the instance OCID as `principalId` for instance principals. No docs page found lists Generative AI-specific audit event types. Source: https://docs.cloud.oracle.com/Content/Audit/Tasks/settingretentionperiod.htm ; https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/Identity/Tasks/callingservicesfrominstances.htm
- **Customer-managed keys are available for fine-tuning data.** Fine-tuning training data can be additionally encrypted with customer-managed OCI Vault keys. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/data-handling.htm
- **Compliance attestations that name Generative AI.** ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (covering OCI Generative AI and Generative AI Agents), Singapore MTCS SS 584 Level 3, the EU Cloud Code of Conduct (Generative AI and Generative AI Agents) and CSA AI STAR are listed in Oracle or third-party attestation material. Get per-service SOC, ISO 27001, FedRAMP, HIPAA and PCI scope from Oracle's compliance pages. (secondary) Source: https://blogs.oracle.com/cloud-infrastructure/raising-the-bar-for-trustworthy-ai-at-oracle ; https://eucoc.cloud/fileadmin/cloud-coc/files/reports/202607_ReportVerificationDoA_Oracle_2022LVL02SCOPE4214vf.pdf ; https://www.oracle.com/it/corporate/cloud-compliance/
- **Generative AI reached US government clouds on 2026-08-11.** It became available in US Gov West (Phoenix) and the US Defense Cloud. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/services/generative-ai/

## 5. Commercial model
- **OCI is billed through Universal Credits.** Annual Universal Credits bill a 12-month pool in advance, debited monthly by usage, with unused credit forfeited. Monthly Universal Credit is a monthly commitment, with overage billed in arrears. Universal Credits Pay As You Go has no up-front fee and bills metered usage monthly in arrears at list price or rate card, whichever is lower. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/get-started/subscriptions-cloud/ocpib/billing-models-offered.html
- **Generative AI on-demand inference is billed per transaction.** The docs define 1 character = 1 transaction (chat = prompt plus response characters; embeddings = input characters), priced per 10,000 transactions. Check each model's "On-Demand Mode" pricing product, because some models may now be priced per token (unverified). Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/pay-on-demand.htm ; https://www.oracle.com/cloud/price-list/#genai
- **Generative AI dedicated AI clusters are billed per unit-hour.** Hosting clusters have a minimum commitment of 744 unit-hours each; fine-tuning has a minimum of 1 unit-hour per job (some models need 2 units). Imported models are exempt from the 744-hour minimum, and some models apply a hosting multiplier (for example 2x for Command R+ 08-2024). Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/generative-ai/pay-dedicated.htm
- **Generative AI Agents is metered on three items.** Agent usage and data ingestion are billed per 10,000 transactions, and knowledge base storage per gigabyte-hour. No prices were retrieved (the pricing page returned 403 to the fetcher). (secondary: search snippet of the Oracle page) Source: https://www.oracle.com/artificial-intelligence/generative-ai/agents/pricing
- **Cost tooling.** Oracle Cost Advisor for AI has been in Limited Availability since 2026-10-27. The OCI cost estimator has an OCI Generative AI estimator under AI and Machine Learning. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/services/generative-ai/ ; https://www.oracle.com/cloud/costestimator.html
- **Multicloud database services are billed through the hyperscaler marketplace.** Oracle Database@AWS and Oracle Database@Azure are bought as AWS or Azure Marketplace private offers (or public offers for some services, such as Autonomous Database on AWS). Source: https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/database-at-aws/oaaws-gs-private-offer.htm ; https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/oracle/oracle-db/onboard-oracle-database

## Product status and renames relevant to access
- **Enterprise AI Agents in OCI Generative AI went GA on 2026-03-31.** The release added the OCI Responses API, Projects, hosted Applications, Vector Stores, NL2SQL and an "OCI Enterprise AI API Key". Oracle marketing now also uses the "OCI Enterprise AI" brand, while the docs service name is still "OCI Generative AI". Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/generative-ai/enterprise-ai.htm
- **The separate OCI Generative AI Agents service is still documented.** Its own IAM family is `genai-agent-family`. Its latest release note is "Select Models for Agents" (2026-02-05), and no deprecation is announced. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/services/generative-ai-agents/
- **`GenerateText` and `SummarizeText` are no longer available.** They were deprecated 2025-06-26 and became unavailable 2026-06-26; use the chat and Responses APIs instead. Cohere `command-latest` and `command-plus-latest` aliases were removed on 2026-07-30. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/services/generative-ai/
- **Two identity domain retirements.** Managing IDCS federated resources in the OCI Console has been deprecated since 2026-03-18, and the legacy local user `DefaultLocalUser` has been being retired since 2026-05-18. Source: https://docs.oracle.com/iaas/releasenotes/services/identity/
