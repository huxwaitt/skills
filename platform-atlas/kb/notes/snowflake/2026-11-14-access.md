# Snowflake: access, identity, roles, governance and commercial model (as of 2026-11-14)

## 1. Getting access
- **A Snowflake organization groups a customer's accounts and is created by Snowflake, not the customer.** An organization is a first-class object linking all accounts owned by one business entity, used for account management, billing, replication/failover and data sharing; Snowflake creates it when the first account is provisioned, after which more accounts can be created self-service. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/organizations
- **An organization can contain a Snowflake organization account, regular accounts (including trials) and Snowflake Open Catalog accounts.** The organization account is a special account for organization administrators and for premium views in the `ORGANIZATION_USAGE` schema; it became GA on 2025-01-27. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/organizations ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/2025/other/2025-01-27-org-account
- **Each Snowflake account lives on one cloud (AWS, Microsoft Azure or Google Cloud) in one region, chosen independently per account.** Credit and storage prices vary by region and platform, and mixing platforms can add data-transfer charges. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/intro-cloud-platforms
- **Snowflake editions are Standard, Enterprise, Business Critical and Virtual Private Snowflake (VPS), each a superset of the previous one.** Enterprise adds multi-cluster warehouses, Time Travel up to 90 days, masking and row access policies; Business Critical adds PrivateLink, Tri-Secret Secure, HIPAA/PCI DSS support, failover/failback and client redirect; VPS runs in a fully separate environment with no Marketplace listings. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/intro-editions
- **Cortex AI Functions, Cortex Analyst, Cortex Fine-tuning and Cortex Search are listed for all four Snowflake editions.** The editions page shows no edition gating for these Cortex features (but Cortex AI Guardrails needs Enterprise or higher, see section 4). Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/intro-editions
- **The preferred Snowflake account identifier is `orgname-account_name`; the account locator form is legacy.** URLs are `https://<orgname>-<account_name>.snowflakecomputing.com` (or `<locator>.<cloud_region_id>.<cloud>.snowflakecomputing.com`); account names may contain underscores and Snowflake also accepts the hyphenated variant, which is required for Okta SSO and SCIM. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/admin-account-identifier
- **Snowflake self-service trials last 30 days or until the free balance runs out, then the account is suspended.** AI features are disabled by default on self-service trials and are enabled by adding a credit card; upgrading moves the account to On Demand billing. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/admin-trial-account
- **Day-one asks for an engineer on a Snowflake engagement.** Ask for: the account identifier (`orgname-account_name`) and URL (public or PrivateLink); a PERSON user with SSO and MFA; a custom role with the needed `SNOWFLAKE.CORTEX_*` database roles and `USAGE` on the target database, schema and warehouse; a SERVICE or SERVICE_AGENT user for the agent runtime with its auth method (key pair, WIF or PAT plus a network policy); the value of `CORTEX_ENABLED_CROSS_REGION`; and whether `CORTEX_USER` and `USE AI FUNCTIONS` were revoked from PUBLIC. (Synthesis of the sources in sections 2 to 4.) Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-agents-setup ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cross-region-inference

## 2. Identity and authentication
- **Snowflake user `TYPE` values are `PERSON`, `SERVICE`, `LEGACY_SERVICE`, `SERVICE_AGENT`, `SNOWFLAKE_SERVICE` and `NULL` (treated as PERSON).** PERSON users are humans who must enroll in MFA; SNOWFLAKE_SERVICE users are created by Snowflake for Snowpark Container Services and cannot be created or assigned by admins. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/admin-user-management
- **Snowflake `TYPE = SERVICE` users cannot use passwords or MFA.** They cannot have `FIRST_NAME`, `LAST_NAME`, `PASSWORD`, `MUST_CHANGE_PASSWORD` or `MINS_TO_BYPASS_MFA`, are not subject to authentication-policy MFA enforcement, and authenticate with key pair, OAuth, workload identity federation or programmatic access tokens. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/admin-user-management
- **Snowflake `TYPE = SERVICE_AGENT` is a user type for an autonomous AI agent acting under its own identity.** It cannot use passwords or SAML SSO, supports workload identity federation, key pair and programmatic access tokens, can be managed via SQL or SCIM (`type = service_agent`), and every session it opens is agent-active (`SYS_CONTEXT('SNOWFLAKE$CURRENT','IS_AGENT_ACTIVATED')` returns `'TRUE'`). Release status is not stated on the page. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/admin-user-management ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/agent-identity
- **Snowflake agent identity distinguishes delegated agents (acting for a user) from autonomous agents (own SERVICE_AGENT identity).** Cortex Agents, Cortex lite agents, CoCo clients, Snowflake CoWork and Snowflake-managed MCP servers are marked agentic automatically; custom Snowflake OAuth or External OAuth integrations are marked agentic with `IS_AGENTIC = TRUE`; a PAT alone does not make a delegated session agentic. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/agent-identity
- **Snowflake OAuth token exchange issues per-task delegated tokens for agents (RFC 8693).** POST to `https://<account_identifier>.snowflakecomputing.com/oauth/token` with `grant_type=urn:ietf:params:oauth:grant-type:token-exchange`, a `subject_token` (OAuth access token, PAT or WIF), optional `scope=session:role:<role>`, `authorization_details` with a Restricted Session Scope (`snowflake-rss.v1`) and `act={"sub":"...","type":"agent"}`; tokens last one hour by default, have no refresh token and cannot be re-exchanged. Status not stated. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/oauth-token-exchange
- **Snowflake does not authenticate the caller of token exchange and does not verify `act`.** Anyone holding a valid subject token can exchange it, so the subject token must stay in the trusted app tier; `act` is self-asserted and must not be used for access control. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/oauth-token-exchange
- **Single-factor password sign-in is being retired in three phases.** Phase 1 (Sep 2025 to Jan 2026, bundle 2025_06): MFA required for password sign-in to Snowsight; Phase 2 (May to Jul 2026, rolling): new human users need a second factor on all surfaces and new non-human users must be `SERVICE`; Phase 3 (Aug to Oct 2026, rolling): no human password-only sign-in and non-human users are blocked from passwords. Dates vary per account and may be extended. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/security-mfa-rollout
- **Snowflake `LEGACY_SERVICE` is deprecated.** After Phase 2 it cannot be assigned in `CREATE USER` or `ALTER USER`; in Phase 3 (Aug to Oct 2026) existing LEGACY_SERVICE users are migrated to `SERVICE` and blocked from passwords. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/security-mfa-rollout ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/admin-user-management
- **Snowflake MFA methods are passkeys (recommended), TOTP authenticator apps and Duo.** Enforce with an authentication policy using `MFA_ENROLLMENT = 'REQUIRED'`, restrict methods with `MFA_POLICY (ALLOWED_METHODS = ...)`, and require MFA after SSO with `MFA_POLICY (ENFORCE_MFA_ON_EXTERNAL_AUTHENTICATION = 'ALL')`; `ALLOW_CLIENT_MFA_CACHING` caches MFA tokens up to four hours. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/security-mfa
- **Snowflake authentication policies control `AUTHENTICATION_METHODS`, `CLIENT_TYPES`, `SECURITY_INTEGRATIONS`, MFA, `PAT_POLICY` and `WORKLOAD_IDENTITY_POLICY`.** A user-level policy overrides the account-level one; creating needs `CREATE AUTHENTICATION POLICY` on a schema, applying needs SECURITYADMIN or `APPLY AUTHENTICATION POLICY`; by default all methods and clients are allowed; `CLIENT_TYPES` is best-effort and does not restrict REST API access. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/authentication-policies ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/workload-identity-federation
- **Snowflake human SSO supports SAML 2.0 (`TYPE = SAML2`) and OIDC sign-in.** Okta and Microsoft Entra ID have native SAML support, other SAML IdPs need a custom app; managed OIDC providers are Google and Entra ID, custom OIDC includes Okta, PingFederate, Auth0 and Keycloak; drivers use `authenticator=OAUTH_AUTHORIZATION_CODE` for OIDC SSO, not `externalbrowser`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/admin-security-fed-auth-overview
- **Snowflake SCIM provisioning supports Okta (`OKTA_PROVISIONER`), Microsoft Entra ID (`AAD_PROVISIONER`) and custom IdPs (`GENERIC_SCIM_PROVISIONER`).** Create with `CREATE SECURITY INTEGRATION ... TYPE = SCIM SCIM_CLIENT = 'okta' RUN_AS_ROLE = 'OKTA_PROVISIONER'`; the provisioner role must own the users and roles it manages and needs `CREATE USER` and `CREATE ROLE`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/scim-intro ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/scim-okta
- **Snowflake key pair authentication needs at least a 2048-bit RSA key.** Two public keys (`RSA_PUBLIC_KEY`, `RSA_PUBLIC_KEY_2`) allow rotation; named key pairs (`ALTER USER ... ADD KEY PAIR`) are now recommended because `RSA_PUBLIC_KEY` supports neither role restriction nor expiration; verify with `DESC USER` `RSA_PUBLIC_KEY_FP`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/key-pair-auth
- **Snowflake workload identity federation (WIF) gives SERVICE and SERVICE_AGENT users keyless auth from AWS IAM roles, Microsoft Entra ID / Azure managed identities, Google Cloud service accounts and OIDC issuers.** OIDC covers EKS, AKS, GKE, GitHub Actions and GitLab (customised `sub` claim) and SPIFFE/SPIRE; configure with `CREATE USER ... WORKLOAD_IDENTITY = (TYPE = AWS|AZURE|GCP|OIDC ...)`; drivers use `authenticator='WORKLOAD_IDENTITY'` plus `workload_identity_provider`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/workload-identity-federation
- **Configuring WIF or PATs on another user needs `OWNERSHIP` or `MODIFY PROGRAMMATIC AUTHENTICATION METHODS` on that user.** Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/workload-identity-federation ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/programmatic-access-tokens
- **Snowflake programmatic access tokens (PATs) work for PERSON, SERVICE and SERVICE_AGENT users.** They authenticate the REST APIs, SQL API, SCIM API, Snowpark Container Services and Horizon Catalog endpoint, and replace the password in drivers; REST calls send `Authorization: Bearer <token>` with optional `X-Snowflake-Authorization-Token-Type: PROGRAMMATIC_ACCESS_TOKEN`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/programmatic-access-tokens
- **Snowflake PAT lifetime defaults to 15 days with a 365-day maximum, and each user can hold up to 15 tokens.** Set per token with `DAYS_TO_EXPIRY`, per policy with `PAT_POLICY` `DEFAULT_EXPIRY_IN_DAYS` / `MAX_EXPIRY_IN_DAYS`; manage with `ALTER USER ... ADD | ROTATE | REMOVE PROGRAMMATIC ACCESS TOKEN`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/programmatic-access-tokens
- **Snowflake SERVICE users must be subject to a network policy to generate or use a PAT; SERVICE_AGENT users need none.** PERSON users can generate a PAT without one but need one to authenticate; `ROLE_RESTRICTION` is required by default for SERVICE and SERVICE_AGENT tokens, and `BLOCKED_ROLES_LIST` can stop roles such as ACCOUNTADMIN being used. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/programmatic-access-tokens
- **Snowflake OAuth (`TYPE = OAUTH`) uses the authorization code flow with browser consent; External OAuth (`TYPE = EXTERNAL_OAUTH`) suits programmatic clients.** Snowflake OAuth supports partner clients (Tableau, Looker, Alation, ThoughtSpot) and `OAUTH_CLIENT = CUSTOM`; its documented grant types are only `authorization_code` and `refresh_token`, and access tokens last about 10 minutes. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/oauth-intro ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/oauth-snowflake-overview ; https://docs.snowflake.com/user-guide/oauth-custom
- **Snowflake External OAuth supports Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, PingFederate and custom authorization servers, and maps roles from scopes.** Scopes are `session:role:<role>` or `session:role-any` (needs `EXTERNAL_OAUTH_ANY_ROLE_MODE`); users map via `EXTERNAL_OAUTH_TOKEN_USER_MAPPING_CLAIM` and `EXTERNAL_OAUTH_SNOWFLAKE_USER_MAPPING_ATTRIBUTE`; it is the documented path for client-credentials machine-to-machine flows. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/oauth-ext-overview
- **Both Snowflake OAuth and External OAuth block ACCOUNTADMIN, ORGADMIN, GLOBALORGADMIN and SECURITYADMIN by default.** Unblock with `OAUTH_ADD_PRIVILEGED_ROLES_TO_BLOCKED_LIST = FALSE` or `EXTERNAL_OAUTH_ADD_PRIVILEGED_ROLES_TO_BLOCKED_LIST = FALSE`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/oauth-snowflake-overview ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/oauth-ext-overview
- **Snowflake added a built-in `SNOWFLAKE$LOCAL_APPLICATION` OAuth integration for local applications (release note 2026-01-21).** Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/2026/other/2026-01-21-snowflake-oauth-local-applications ; https://docs.snowflake.com/release-notes/bcr-bundles/un-bundled/bcr-2056
- **Snowflake REST APIs (including Cortex Agents) accept PATs, key-pair JWTs and OAuth; `X-Snowflake-Role` sets the role, otherwise the user's default role applies.** Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-agents-setup
- **The Snowflake-managed MCP server defaults to Snowflake OAuth, supports External OAuth, and accepts PATs.** Snowflake recommends OAuth over hard-coded tokens and a least-privileged role for any PAT; OAuth sessions use the user's `DEFAULT_ROLE`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-agents-mcp

## 3. Roles and permissions
- **Snowflake combines discretionary access control (owners), role-based access control and user-based access control.** In UBAC, privileges granted directly to users count only when `USE SECONDARY ROLES` is `ALL`; a session has one primary role (which alone authorizes `CREATE`) plus any number of secondary roles. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/security-access-control-overview
- **Snowflake system roles are GLOBALORGADMIN, ORGADMIN, ACCOUNTADMIN, SECURITYADMIN, USERADMIN, SYSADMIN and PUBLIC.** ACCOUNTADMIN contains SYSADMIN and SECURITYADMIN; SECURITYADMIN holds `MANAGE GRANTS` and inherits USERADMIN (`CREATE USER`, `CREATE ROLE`); SYSADMIN creates warehouses and databases; PUBLIC is granted to every user and role. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/security-access-control-overview
- **Snowflake GLOBALORGADMIN in the organization account replaces ORGADMIN, which is being phased out for multi-account organizations.** ORGADMIN can be enabled in up to eight accounts by default (`ALTER ACCOUNT <name> SET IS_ORG_ADMIN = TRUE`); Snowflake promises at least three months' email notice before removing it; no removal date was published as of 2026-11-14. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/organization-administrators
- **Snowflake recommends custom roles rolled up to SYSADMIN and created by USERADMIN.** Without the roll-up, SYSADMIN cannot manage objects owned by the custom role. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/security-access-control-overview
- **Snowflake database roles are scoped to one database and cannot be activated directly or granted directly to users.** Grant them to an account role. Snowflake Native App providers define application roles; `SNOWFLAKE.PUBLIC` and `SNOWFLAKE.APP_PUBLIC` are managed automatically. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/security-access-control-overview
- **Calling Snowflake Cortex AI Functions needs the account privilege `USE AI FUNCTIONS` (or per-function `USE AI FUNCTION <name>`) plus database role `SNOWFLAKE.CORTEX_USER` or `SNOWFLAKE.AI_FUNCTIONS_USER`.** `USE AI FUNCTIONS` is granted to PUBLIC by default and managed by ACCOUNTADMIN (for example `REVOKE USE AI FUNCTIONS ON ACCOUNT FROM ROLE PUBLIC;`); the privilege was introduced in a 2026-02-20 release note, and per-function privileges reached GA on 2026-05-13. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/aisql-privileges-and-access ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/2026/other/2026-02-20-use-ai-functions-privilege
- **Snowflake `SNOWFLAKE.CORTEX_USER` is granted to PUBLIC by default and covers all Covered AI Features.** These include Cortex Agents, AI Functions, Cortex Search and CoWork; revoke with `REVOKE DATABASE ROLE SNOWFLAKE.CORTEX_USER FROM ROLE PUBLIC;`. Revoking does not block ACCOUNTADMIN. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/aisql-privileges-and-access ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/opting-out
- **Snowflake `SNOWFLAKE.AI_FUNCTIONS_USER` (GA 2026-04-02) covers scalar AI functions only, excluding `AI_AGG` and `AI_SUMMARIZE_AGG`, and is not granted to PUBLIC.** Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/aisql-privileges-and-access ; https://docs.snowflake.com/release-notes/2026/other/2026-04-02-ai-functions-user-db-role
- **Snowflake `SNOWFLAKE.CORTEX_EMBED_USER` covers `AI_EMBED`, `EMBED_TEXT_768`, `EMBED_TEXT_1024` and Cortex Search services with managed embeddings, and is not granted to PUBLIC.** Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/aisql-privileges-and-access
- **Snowflake model-level RBAC uses application roles in the SNOWFLAKE application, such as `SNOWFLAKE."CORTEX-MODEL-ROLE-ALL"` and per-model `CORTEX-MODEL-ROLE-<model>`.** Model objects live in `SNOWFLAKE.MODELS` and are refreshed daily by `SNOWFLAKE.MODELS.CORTEX_BASE_MODELS_REFRESH_TASK` (on demand: `CALL SNOWFLAKE.MODELS.CORTEX_BASE_MODELS_REFRESH();`); accounts with allowlist `'All'` get `CORTEX-MODEL-ROLE-ALL` via `SNOWFLAKE.PUBLIC`, removable only with `CALL SNOWFLAKE.LOCAL.REVOKE_FROM_PUBLIC_APPLICATION_ROLE('APP_ROLE','CORTEX-MODEL-ROLE-ALL');`. ACCOUNTADMIN always has model access. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/aisql-privileges-and-access ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/bcr-bundles/un-bundled/bcr-2378
- **Snowflake `CORTEX_MODELS_ALLOWLIST` is being retired in favour of model RBAC.** From 2026-08-05 it can only be set to `'None'`; a one-time migration ran 2026-08-17 to 2026-09-04; allowlist-based control was removed 2026-09-08 via bundle 2026_07, which RBAC-enforces embedding models (AI_EMBED, Cortex Search); full retirement January 2027. Dates are subject to change. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/bcr-bundles/un-bundled/bcr-2378
- **Calling Snowflake Cortex Agents needs `SNOWFLAKE.CORTEX_USER` or `SNOWFLAKE.CORTEX_AGENT_USER`; CORTEX_AGENT_USER excludes Cortex Analyst, so add `SNOWFLAKE.CORTEX_ANALYST_USER` for Analyst tools.** Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-agents-setup
- **Snowflake Cortex Agent object privileges are `CREATE AGENT` (schema), `USAGE`, `MODIFY`, `MONITOR` and `OWNERSHIP` (agent).** USAGE also needs USAGE on the database and schema; MONITOR shows threads, logs and traces; `CREATE OR REPLACE` needs OWNERSHIP; temporary agents do not need `CREATE AGENT`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-agents-setup ; https://docs.snowflake.com/sql-reference/sql/create-agent
- **Snowflake Cortex Agent tools need the caller's own grants.** These are USAGE on Cortex Search services, USAGE on the database, schema and tables behind semantic views, USAGE on custom-tool functions and procedures, and USAGE on warehouses in the agent spec; by default (`orchestration.tool_not_accessible = accept`) inaccessible tools are skipped with warnings, while `reject` fails the run. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-agents-setup
- **Snowflake Cortex Analyst needs `SNOWFLAKE.CORTEX_USER` or `SNOWFLAKE.CORTEX_ANALYST_USER`.** It also needs SELECT on referenced tables, USAGE on referenced Cortex Search services, and READ/WRITE on the stage holding a YAML semantic model; ACCOUNTADMIN can disable it account-wide with `ALTER ACCOUNT SET ENABLE_CORTEX_ANALYST = FALSE`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-analyst
- **Creating a Snowflake Cortex Search service needs `CREATE CORTEX SEARCH SERVICE` on the schema, SELECT on sources, USAGE on the refresh warehouse, and CORTEX_USER or CORTEX_EMBED_USER; querying needs USAGE on the service, database and schema.** Services search with owner's rights; `OPERATE` suspends/resumes and `MONITOR` shows refresh history. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-search/cortex-search-overview
- **Snowflake Intelligence was renamed Snowflake CoWork (and Cortex Code renamed Snowflake CoCo) at Snowflake Summit, June 2026.** SQL keywords still use `SNOWFLAKE INTELLIGENCE`, and the `SNOWFLAKE_INTELLIGENCE.AGENTS` schema is deprecated for visibility in favour of the CoWork object. (secondary for the rename date) Source: https://itbrief.com.au/story/snowflake-renames-ai-tools-amid-broader-platform-push ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/snowflake-cowork/deploy-agents
- **The Snowflake CoWork object `SNOWFLAKE_INTELLIGENCE_OBJECT_DEFAULT` (one per account) controls which agents CoWork shows.** `CREATE SNOWFLAKE INTELLIGENCE ON ACCOUNT` is held by ACCOUNTADMIN by default; grant `USAGE ON SNOWFLAKE INTELLIGENCE SNOWFLAKE_INTELLIGENCE_OBJECT_DEFAULT`; add agents with `ALTER SNOWFLAKE INTELLIGENCE ... ADD AGENT <db.schema.agent>`. Agents are not added automatically. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/snowflake-cowork/deploy-agents
- **Snowflake CoWork users need USAGE on the agent, its database and schema, plus tool grants, a default role and a default warehouse.** Tool grants are USAGE on search services and semantic views, READ on stages for citations, and SELECT on tables; restrict a user to CoWork with `ALTER USER ... SET ALLOWED_INTERFACES = (SNOWFLAKE_INTELLIGENCE)` or the SCIM attribute `allowedInterfaces`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/snowflake-cowork/deploy-agents
- **Snowflake CoWork Automations' `EXECUTE AGENT TASK` is granted to PUBLIC by default, while MANAGE ACCESS on Artifacts stops being granted to any role by default from late September 2026.** Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/release-notes/bcr-bundles/un-bundled/bcr-2349 ; https://docs.snowflake.com/release-notes/bcr-bundles/un-bundled/bcr-manage-access-dashboard-artifacts
- **Snowflake-managed MCP server privileges are `CREATE MCP SERVER`, `OWNERSHIP`, `MODIFY` and `USAGE`, and server USAGE does not grant tool access.** Each tool needs its own grant, such as USAGE on a Cortex Search service, SELECT on a semantic view or USAGE on an agent; the endpoint is `https://<account_url>/api/v2/databases/{db}/schemas/{schema}/mcp-servers/{name}`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-agents-mcp
- **With Restricted Caller's Rights, `USE AI FUNCTIONS` must be granted to both the session role and the service or application owner role.** Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/aisql-privileges-and-access
- **Least-privilege setup for a Snowflake agent runtime identity.** The setup: a SERVICE_AGENT (or SERVICE) user with WIF or key pair; a custom role holding `SNOWFLAKE.CORTEX_AGENT_USER` (plus `CORTEX_ANALYST_USER` if needed) and only the tool, warehouse, database and schema USAGE/SELECT grants; set as the PAT `ROLE_RESTRICTION` or OAuth scope; with ACCOUNTADMIN revoking `CORTEX_USER` and `USE AI FUNCTIONS` from PUBLIC; USERADMIN creates the user and role, SECURITYADMIN or the object owner grants. (Synthesis.) Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-agents-setup ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/programmatic-access-tokens ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/security-access-control-overview
- **Snowflake data protection policies can check `IS_AGENT_ACTIVATED`, and Restricted Session Scope caps agent privileges.** Masking, row access, projection, aggregation and join policies can hide data from agent sessions; `AGENT_RESTRICTED_SESSION_SCOPE` on a session policy sets a privilege ceiling that applies only when an agent is active. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/agent-identity

## 4. Governance, security and compliance
- **Snowflake network policies combine schema-level network rules into allow and block lists for inbound access.** Rules use `ALLOWED_NETWORK_RULE_LIST` / `BLOCKED_NETWORK_RULE_LIST` with rule types including `IPV4`, `IPV6`, `AWSVPCEID`, `AZURELINKID` and `COMPUTE_POOL`; block wins; security integration policies override user policies, which override account policies; creating one needs SECURITYADMIN or `CREATE NETWORK POLICY`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/network-policies
- **Snowflake AWS PrivateLink requires Business Critical or higher.** ACCOUNTADMIN authorizes with `SYSTEM$AUTHORIZE_PRIVATELINK` and gets endpoint details from `SYSTEM$GET_PRIVATELINK_CONFIG`; URLs take a `.privatelink.snowflakecomputing.com` form; Azure Private Link and Google Cloud Private Service Connect are documented separately; block public access with a network policy after testing. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/admin-security-privatelink

## 5. Commercial model
- **Snowflake bills consumption in credits; AI features use AI Credits and everything else uses Platform Credits.** AI Credits have one price regardless of edition or region and apply to the features in Table 6 of the Snowflake Service Consumption Table: Cortex Code, Cortex Agents, Snowflake CoWork, Cortex REST API, Cortex AI Gateway, AI Functions, Cortex Search, Cortex Batch Search and AI Parse Doc. Cortex Fine-tuning and the Cortex Analyst API stay on legacy Platform Credits. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/pricing
- **As shown on 2026-11-14, a Snowflake AI Credit costs $2.00 with global routing and $2.20 with regional routing.** Global applies when `CORTEX_ENABLED_CROSS_REGION` is `ANY_REGION`, `AWS_GLOBAL`, `GCP_GLOBAL` or `AZURE_GLOBAL`; regional applies for `DISABLED` or a geography such as `AWS_US`. Capacity discounts do not apply to AI Credits, but automatic ACV-based AI Credit discounts do. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/pricing
- **The Snowflake Service Consumption Table is the rate card for credits per million tokens, per message and per GB-month.** Its URL is https://www.snowflake.com/legal-files/CreditConsumptionTable.pdf; the PDF could not be parsed here, so no per-model rates are quoted. A secondary source dates it 2026-09-30. Source: https://www.snowflake.com/legal-files/CreditConsumptionTable.pdf
- **Snowflake Platform Credit price varies by edition and region, and accounts buy On Demand or Capacity (pre-purchased) plans.** The docs' illustration (undated) uses $3.00 per Platform Credit for Enterprise. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/pricing ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/cost-understanding-overall
- **Snowflake Gen1 standard warehouses burn 1 credit/hour at X-Small, doubling per size up to 512 at 6X-Large, billed per second with a 60-second minimum.** Gen2 warehouse rates are in the Service Consumption Table. Snowflake recommends warehouses no larger than MEDIUM for AI Functions because larger sizes do not help. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/warehouses-overview ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/aisql-cost
- **Snowflake Cortex AI Functions are billed per million tokens (input and output), monitored in `CORTEX_AI_FUNCTIONS_USAGE_HISTORY` and `METERING_DAILY_HISTORY` with `SERVICE_TYPE = 'AI_SERVICES'`.** The older cost page names the view `CORTEX_FUNCTIONS_USAGE_HISTORY` and `CORTEX_FUNCTIONS_QUERY_USAGE_HISTORY`; the newer governance page uses `CORTEX_AI_FUNCTIONS_USAGE_HISTORY`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/aisql-cost ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/governance-and-availability/ai-cost-management-and-governance
- **Snowflake Cortex Agents bill orchestration by tokens plus each tool's own cost.** Analyst counts per token when called via Agents; Search bills index serving; custom tools bill warehouse time; web search, code execution, MCP connectors and skills can add cost; usage is in `CORTEX_AGENT_USAGE_HISTORY`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-agents ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/governance-and-availability/ai-cost-management-and-governance
- **Snowflake Cortex Analyst bills per successful (HTTP 200) message, plus warehouse time for the generated SQL.** Usage is in `CORTEX_ANALYST_USAGE_HISTORY`. Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-analyst
- **Snowflake Cortex Search bills serving per GB-month of uncompressed indexed data while the service is available, plus embedding tokens for changed rows, refresh warehouse time and storage.** Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-search/cortex-search-overview
- **Snowflake CoWork usage is recorded in `SNOWFLAKE_COWORK_USAGE_HISTORY` (service type `SNOWFLAKE_INTELLIGENCE`) in tokens and tools.** Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/governance-and-availability/ai-cost-management-and-governance

Source: https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/budgets/per-user-quotas ; https://docs.snowflake.com/en/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/governance-and-availability/ai-cost-management-and-governance
